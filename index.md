# Privacy Policy — Kepta

**Last updated: 26 July 2026**

Kepta is a notification archive that runs entirely on your
phone. This policy explains exactly what it stores, where it stays, and what it
never does.

Kepta is published by **Imperfect** (Perfecto Gardoce). Because no data ever leaves
your device, the developer has no access to anything Kepta records.

---

## The short version

Kepta stores your notifications **only on your own device**. Nothing is uploaded,
nothing is shared, and there is no account, server, or analytics of any kind. The
developer cannot see your data, because it never leaves your phone.

---

## What Kepta reads

To do its job, Kepta uses Android's **Notification Access** permission. Android
delivers every notification your phone shows to any app holding that permission,
so Kepta technically receives all of them.

By default it only **saves** notifications that are actual messages — those an app
sends using Android's messaging notification format. Everything else, including
games, promotions, delivery updates and system alerts, is discarded immediately and
never written to storage.

Messaging apps are recorded automatically so that a conversation is not lost before
you notice the app exists. You can switch off any app, or any individual
conversation, in **Settings → Apps**. Turning one off stops all further recording
from it.

For the apps you enable, Kepta stores:

- Message text
- Sender name, as shown in the notification
- Conversation name
- The date and time the message was sent
- The name of the app that sent it
- Preview images, when an app attaches one to its notification
- Profile pictures, when a notification carries one, so a conversation can be
  recognised at a glance
- Whether the sender later deleted the message
- A copy of the app's icon, so conversations can be identified offline

## Alerts, if you ask for them

Non-message notifications — game announcements, promotions, delivery updates,
system notices — are **never recorded unless you switch them on** for a specific app
in **Settings → Apps**. They are off for every app until you say otherwise.

When you do switch one on, Kepta stores that app's notification title, body text,
the time it arrived, and any attached image. These are kept in a separate **Alerts**
tab, apart from your conversations, and are covered by the same retention settings
and the same delete controls as everything else. Switching the app back off stops
all further recording from it.

## What Kepta never does

- **No internet.** The app has no network permission and makes no network requests.
- **No account or sign-in.**
- **No analytics, telemetry, or crash reporting.**
- **No advertising** and no advertising identifiers.
- **No data sharing or selling.** There is no third party to share with.
- **No access to your accounts.** Kepta cannot read your messaging apps' data,
  only the notifications Android shows on screen.
- **No scanning of your installed apps.** Kepta does not request permission to list
  the software on your phone. It learns an app's name and icon only from the
  notification that app sends.
- **No location, contacts, camera, microphone, or file access.**

## Where your data is stored

Everything is written to Kepta's private storage area on your device, which
Android isolates from other apps. Preview images and cached app icons are saved as
files in that same private area.

## What you control

- **Which apps are recorded.** Messaging apps start enabled; switch off any you
  do not want kept.
- **Whether alerts are recorded at all.** Off for every app until you turn it on.
- **Individual conversations.** You can stop recording a single conversation
  without disabling the whole app.
- **How long anything is kept** (see below).
- **Deletion**, at any level: one message, one conversation, all photos, or
  everything.

## How long it is kept

You control this in **Settings → Storage and retention**. By default, message text
is kept for one year and photos for 30 days. Anything older is deleted
automatically. You can change both, delete all photos, delete a single
conversation, or erase everything at any time.

Uninstalling Kepta permanently deletes everything it stored.

## Sharing and exporting

Kepta can hand a single message or photo to Android's system share sheet, and can
export a conversation as a text file. When you do this, **you** choose the
destination app, and the content leaves Kepta's control at that point — it is then
governed by whatever app you sent it to. Kepta never shares anything on its own,
and nothing is shared unless you explicitly ask for it.

## Protecting your archive

Kepta can require your fingerprint or screen lock before opening
(**Settings → App lock**), and can blank its preview in the Recents screen. Both are
off by default and are yours to enable.

Because Kepta holds private conversations, turning on App lock is strongly
recommended if other people have physical access to your phone.

## Other people's messages

Kepta saves notifications sent to **you**. Those notifications often contain
messages written by other people, who have not agreed to being archived. Please
use it responsibly, respect the privacy of the people who message you, and follow
the laws in your country about recording communications.

Kepta is not designed to monitor another person, and must not be used that way.

## Age

Kepta is intended for adults (18 and over). It is not directed at children, and
handling other people's private messages responsibly requires adult judgement.

## Your rights

Since Kepta holds no data outside your device, there is nothing for the developer
to disclose, correct, export, or delete on your behalf — you already hold complete
control through the app's own settings. Uninstalling removes everything.
